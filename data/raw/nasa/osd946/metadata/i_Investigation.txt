STUDY
Study Identifier	OSD-946
Study Title	Exercise Training Attenuates the Muscle Mitochondria Genomic Response to Bed Rest (miRNA microarray data)
Study Description	Exercise training during the National Aeronautics and Space Administration 70-d bed rest study effectively counteracted the decline in aerobic capacity, muscle mass, strength, and endurance. We aimed to characterize the genomic response of the participants' vastus lateralis on day 64 of bed rest with and without exercise countermeasures. Twenty-two healthy young males were randomized into three groups: 1) bed rest only (n equals 7), 2) bed rest + aerobic (6 d/wk) and resistance training (3 d/wk) on standard equipment (n equals 7), and 3) bed rest + aerobic and resistance training using a flywheel device (n equals 8). The vastus lateralis gene and microRNA microarrays were analyzed using GeneSpring GX 14.9.1 (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA). This study hosts the microRNA microarray dataset. Gene expression microarray datasets are available in OSD-942.

Study Protocol Description	Twenty-two men (32.3 +/- 6.2 years, 178.3 +/- 6.3 cm, 78.4 +/- 10.1 kg) out of 26 subjects who had completed the intervention and had muscle biopsies to perform genomic analysis were included in this study. Procedures were approved by the institutional review boards at the NASA Johnson Space Center and the University of Texas Medical Branch (UTMB). All subjects provided written informed consent for participation in the bed rest protocols. The study protocol was implemented at the UTMB Institute for Translational Sciences – Clinical Research Center in a dedicated hospital unit referred to as the NASA Flight Analogs Research Unit. A complete description of the subject recruitment, screening and selection criteria can be found in Cromwell et al.. Following consent procedures, subjects were randomized to one of three groups: 1) control; no exercise, bed rest only (CON; n equals 7), 2) bed rest + aerobic and resistance exercise using traditional exercise equipment (EX; n equals 7), and 3) bed rest + aerobic and resistance exercise using a single flywheel device (FLY; n equals 8). Bed Rest and Exercise Training: Baseline data collection was completed with subjects living at the research unit. Subjects were confined to bed rest for 70 days in a 6-degree head-down tilt position and monitored 24 hours daily. Subjects remained at the research facility during the post bed rest data collection. The head-down tilt position was maintained throughout the study except during meals and with aerobic and resistance exercise training (supine or prone horizontal position) and rowing (upright seated position). Exercise was prescribed using the SPRINT protocol consisting of aerobic exercise 6 days per week, 15–35 min/session, and resistance exercise 3 days per week, 35–60 min/session, described in detail previously. In brief, subjects completed alternating days of high-intensity interval (near max intensity) and continuous aerobic training (75% VO2peak) on a supine cycle ergometer and zero-gravity locomotion simulator vertical treadmill respectively. To avoid the known incompatibilities of resistance and aerobic training, these activities occurred concurrently only on the lower intensity continuous aerobic training days. Additionally, training sessions were separated by at least 4 hours. Subjects completed three sets of six to twelve repetitions of four supine lifts: 1) squat, 2) leg press, 3) unilateral leg curl, and 4) heel raise. In the EX group, subjects performed aerobic exercise on a vertical treadmill (high-intensity interval aerobic training) or a supine electronic cycle ergometer (continuous aerobic exercise; Lode B.V., Groningen, the Netherlands). Resistance exercise was conducted on the horizontal leg press (Quantum Fitness, Stafford, TX) and prone leg curl machines (Cybex International, Medway, MA). In the FLY group, subjects completed both aerobic (seated rowing) and resistance exercise (supine) on a single custom-built flywheel device. The squat exercise could not be performed on the flywheel; therefore to maintain consistent exercise volume, the number of sets was increased from three to four for the following three exercises: 1) heel raise, 2) hamstring curl, and 3) leg press. Total RNA was extracted from the VL muscle using TRIzol (Gibco BRL Life Technologies, Rockville, MD) for both gene and microRNA expression analysis. For gene expression only, RNA was purified using the Qiagen-RNeasy Mini Kit. Resuspension of RNA pellets was done in diethyl pyrocarbonate-treated water. Assessment of RNA integrity was performed prior to beginning analytical processing using an Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent Technologies, Inc., Palo Alto, CA, USA). Only samples with an RNA integrity number (RIN) greater than or equals to 9.1 were analyzed.	One hundred ng of total RNA from each sample was labeled with the fluorescent dye Cyanine 3-pCp (Cy3) using the microRNA Labeling Reagent and Hybridization Kit (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA) following the manufacturer’s protocol.	Cy3-labeled RNA was hybridized to Agilent Human microRNA Version 2 Microarray from each sample.	The hybridized array was then washed and scanned according to Agilent specifications and data was extracted from the scanned image using Feature Extraction software version 10.7 (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA).
